# Video Brief: Loss Aversion

## Core Thesis
Losing $100 hurts twice as much as finding $100 feels good. In 1979, Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky discovered that human beings are hardwired with Loss Aversion—a psychological asymmetry where the emotional sting of defeat is more than double the joy of equal gain. This invisible cognitive bias causes people to hold onto bad investments, stay in dead-end routines, and pass up life-changing opportunities just to avoid the temporary discomfort of loss.
