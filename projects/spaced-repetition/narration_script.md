## Complete Script & On-Screen Reveals

### Frame 01: The Hook (0.00s – 7.00s)
```text
You probably forgot most of what you learned yesterday.
```
* **TTS Raw Duration:** ~3.5s
* **Reveal Offset:** 0.35s
* **Composition Window:** 7.00s
* **Visual:** CLOSE-UP: A person's hand confidently closes a book. CUT TO: The same person later, staring at a blank page. The page is empty. Nothing remains. Then — text appears over the void: **YOU THOUGHT YOU LEARNED IT.** Pause. **YOU DIDN'T.**

---

### Frame 02: The Origin (6.50s – 18.00s)
```text
In eighteen eighty-five, a German psychologist named Hermann Ebbinghaus discovered something disturbing about memory.
He tested himself.
Nonsense syllables.
He tried to remember them.
Then he forgot.
And he measured exactly how fast memory dies.
```
* **TTS Raw Duration:** ~12.0s
* **Reveal Offset:** 0.35s
* **Composition Window:** 11.50s
* **Visual:** SLOW PUSH-IN on a vintage desk. Candle flickers. A hand writes in a leather journal. Letters scatter across the page — meaningless combinations. The hand stops. The ink blurs. The letters fade. CUT TO: A close-up of a clock face. The hands move backward. The year **1885** appears etched into glass.

---

### Frame 03: The Mechanism (17.50s – 30.00s)
```text
What he found was devastating.
Without review, you lose seventy percent of new information within twenty-four hours.
By day six, almost nothing remains.
The curve doesn't decline gently.
It falls off a cliff.
```
* **TTS Raw Duration:** ~11.5s
* **Reveal Offset:** 0.35s
* **Composition Window:** 12.50s
* **Visual:** MACRO SHOT: A network of glowing connections — neural pathways. They pulse with light. Then, one by one, they dim. Connections fade. The network becomes sparse. SLOW ZOOM OUT reveals the network is actually drawn on a piece of paper. The paper crumples. CUT TO: A person's face, illuminated by a screen. The screen shows a steep curve descending. The person's expression shifts from focus to realization.

---

### Frame 04: The Reveal (29.50s – 42.00s)
```text
But here's what he discovered next.
If you review right before you forget, the curve resets.
And each time it resets, the decay gets slower.
The memory gets stronger.
```
* **TTS Raw Duration:** ~9.0s
* **Reveal Offset:** 0.35s
* **Composition Window:** 12.50s
* **Visual:** A hand opens a book to the same page. The page is slightly yellowed. The person reads. CLOSE-UP: The words are clear. CUT TO: A calendar. Days marked. A review date. The same book, opened again. The page is brighter. The words are sharper. CUT TO: Another review. The book falls open naturally to the page. The words are permanent now. The decay has slowed. The path is worn.

---

### Frame 05: The Implication (41.50s – 52.00s)
```text
This is why cramming never works.
You flood your brain, but you never let forgetting happen.
Without forgetting, there's no strengthening.
Spacing isn't a hack. It's how your brain learns.
```
* **TTS Raw Duration:** ~10.5s
* **Reveal Offset:** 0.35s
* **Composition Window:** 10.50s
* **Visual:** WIDE SHOT: A library. Desks covered in books. Someone frantically flipping pages. Piles of notes. CUT TO: The same library, later. Most desks empty. One person, calm, reviewing a single page. The room is quiet. The contrast is visual — chaos vs. clarity. The frantic person's notes dissolve. The calm person's page remains.

---

### Frame 06: The Payoff (51.50s – 58.00s)
```text
Forgetting isn't failure.
It's the mechanism.
```
* **TTS Raw Duration:** ~4.0s
* **Reveal Offset:** 0.35s
* **Composition Window:** 6.50s
* **Visual:** CLOSE-UP: A hand places a bookmark in a book. The book closes. CUT TO: The same book, months later. The hand opens it. The bookmark falls to a page full of annotations. The words are still there. The memory held. FINAL FRAME: The book fills the frame. Text overlaid: **FORGETTING ISN'T FAILURE. IT'S THE MECHANISM.**
