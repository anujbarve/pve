# Split-Brain Interpreter (The Illusion of Reason)

## Concept Overview
- **Topic:** The Left-Brain Interpreter & The Illusion of Rational Intent
- **Core Insight:** In landmark split-brain experiments by Michael Gazzaniga, flashing the command "WALK" to the isolated non-verbal right hemisphere causes the subject to stand and walk. When asked why, the verbal left hemisphere instantly confabulates a logical motive ("I wanted to get a soda") rather than admitting it has no idea.
- **Narrative Arc:**
  1. **Shot 01 (The Silent Command):** Dual-optic tachistoscope projection flashes "WALK" into the isolated left visual field; the patient immediately stands and walks.
  2. **Shot 02 (The Severed Bridge):** Neural cross-section reveals the severed corpus callosum; the verbal left hemisphere has zero awareness of the prompt.
  3. **Shot 03 (The Soda Confabulation):** An interrogation audio pulse asks "Why are you walking?"; the left hemisphere instantly invents an elaborate cover story: "I wanted to get a soda."
  4. **Shot 04 (The Left-Brain Interpreter):** Gazzaniga's cognitive mechanism schematic: Unconscious Action (Track 1) triggers first, followed by Post-Hoc Narrative Synthesis (Track 2).
  5. **Shot 05 (The Narrator, Not the Author):** Pull-back into cosmic neural silhouette with gold manifesto typography: "You are the narrator, not the author."
